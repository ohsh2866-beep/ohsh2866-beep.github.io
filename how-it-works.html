How it works

The same pipeline runs every day at noon, Korea time.

Daily production pipeline

  1. Collect the day's topics. Headlines are pulled from Google News RSS and Google Trends for Korea, the United States, and Egypt, in Korean, English, and Arabic. Two of the Korean categories draw instead from a curated library of AI terms and AI services so that explainer content keeps running even on slow news days.
  2. Choose a topic and research it. For each category the most newsworthy topic is selected, then researched further through web search to gather concrete figures, background, and differing viewpoints. Headlines alone are never enough to publish on.
  3. Write the content set. Each topic becomes a blog article of 2,000-3,000 characters, a six-slide card-news set, and a six-scene video script written in spoken, conversational language.
  4. Produce the video. Narration is synthesized with text-to-speech, paired with licensed stock footage or generated background images, subtitled, and mixed with royalty-free background music into a vertical video.
  5. Publish to our own channels. The video goes to YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, the article to the blog. Uploads are spaced out through the day rather than posted all at once.

Editorial rules

  • Only facts confirmed in the collected sources or in follow-up research are published. Figures and causal claims that cannot be verified are left out.
  • Every article ends with a note stating that it summarizes publicly reported information.
  • Content is reviewed by the operator, who can correct or withhold any item before it goes out.
  • Content is labeled as AI-generated wherever the platform offers such a label.
  • Videos use stock footage and royalty-free music. Prompts for generated imagery exclude real people's faces, brand logos, and national flags.
